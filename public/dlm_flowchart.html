⚙️ Prototype Hardware Parameters
Grid Adapter (AC-DC) 12 V, 2 A (Max 24 W)
Solar PV Panel 12 V, 10 W (Max ≈ 0.83 A)
Bus Voltage (Nominal) 12 V DC
EV (Toy Car Battery) Max 1.0 A (≈ 12 W)
Control Device ESP32 Microcontroller
Sensing Channels ACS712 (x2) + V-Divider
Control Interval 1 second (50 Hz ADC)
🏛️ Experimental Sanctioned Limit
Adapter Max Power = 12 V × 2 A = 24 W
Plimit = 18 W (≈ 1.5 A at 12 V)
Why 18 W was Chosen?
  • Allows dynamic control: Keeps a 6 W safety headroom below the 24 W adapter rating.
  • Prevents adapter trip: Protects the 2 A power supply from thermal shutdown under combined load.
  • Keeps system stable: Prevents 12 V bus voltage collapse when household switches flip.
  • Demonstration range: Enables distinct tests from 0.3 W (base LEDs) to 16.2 W (near limit).

📖 Step-by-Step Model Execution
Step 1: Measure
Sensor Sampling
Every second, ESP32 samples bus voltage and current of house, PV panel, and EV.
Step 2: Calculate
Powers & Headroom
Computes Phouse, PPV, Grid Headroom (18W − Phouse), and Solar Surplus.
Step 3: Check Overload
Adapter Safety
If Phouse ≥ 18 W, EV immediately turns OFF to protect the 2 A adapter.
Step 4: Select Mode
3 Operating Modes
User selects Dynamic Grid, Solar Priority, or Hybrid Mode.
Step 5: Current Control
PWM Regulation
Target power is converted to current. If < 0.2 A, EV pauses; else PWM adjusts charging rate.

🔄 Operating Modes in Detail

Mode 1: Dynamic Grid Mode: Uses available grid headroom only. No priority given to PV.

Mode 2: Solar Priority Mode: Uses only solar surplus. If PV < household load, EV halts.

Mode 3: Hybrid Mode: Uses solar surplus first, then remaining grid headroom for maximum speed.
